## Curriculum and difficulty review

Checked against the supplied Australian Curriculum v9 F–6 Mathematics PDF, Year 5 Number content (PDF pages 48–52). Each form samples all ten Year 5 Number descriptor codes. Each slot has the same code, skill, response type and intended demand across the five forms; examples differ. Labels describe author judgement, not empirical calibration or a guarantee that every example will have identical response rates.

The larger multiplication, interpretation of a remainder, and multi-step models supply end-of-Level-5 challenge. Percentage-grid and sequence tasks provide accessible entry points. The assessment does not need every question to be difficult to sample growth meaningfully.

## Changes from the existing post-test

- Five independent example sets replace the previous mismatched forms.
- Short direct questions replace wordy “a student says” prompts without adding reading difficulty to the maths.
- Fractions use stacked notation and no shaded ordering hints. All ordering sets contain one improper fraction and related denominators.
- Bus capacity uses a plausible 48 passenger seats and asks explicitly for enough buses for everyone.
- Budget cards distinguish quantity, price for ONE, money available and room hire. They never show an unexplained cash picture or calculated subtotal.
- Practical problems include a bus illustration, seats, meal and room-hire icons. Mathematical tasks use place-value charts, number lines, grids and clear notation.
- Algorithm step-ordering has been replaced by a short divisibility decision and a simple next-number sequence, reflecting the owner's preference.

## Limits and review focus

Twenty scored responses sample descriptors; they do not establish mastery of every elaboration. These forms do not directly assess student-created algorithms, explanations of chosen strategies, use of digital tools, or decimal-number-line placement. Q19's five examples follow the divisible branch; Q4 supplies a separate divisibility decision. Teacher observation and other program tasks are needed for broader evidence.

Difficulty matching includes number size, denominator relationship, operation steps, response format, amount of visual help and language. Factor/percentage fluency and arithmetic examples can still differ slightly in practice; compare item performance once sufficient school data exists.

The owner should now review clarity, visuals and child usability in Demo Review → Number Nexus → Level 5 → Review all five forms. Return controls preserve the selected realm/year. No student writes occur in this review route.
