# Chance Hollow Level 6 assessment review

Five parallel forms, each containing 20 questions, are available at `/demo-review/chance-level6` for manual review. Current student banks are unchanged pending approval.

Checked against the supplied Australian Curriculum v9 F–6 document (Year 6 Probability) and Chance Hollow's existing six lesson weeks, including probability-scale tasks, probability builders and increasing-trial simulations.

| Questions | Curriculum | Coverage | Week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | AC9M6P01 | Numerical scale, decimal/percentage connections, ordering, contextual meaning | 1 |
| 5–8 | AC9M6P01 | Calculate, complement, construct and estimate probabilities | 2 |
| 9–11 | AC9M6P02 | Expected counts and observed frequencies | 3 |
| 12–14 | AC9M6P02 | Increasing-trial simulation and relative variation | 4 |
| 15 | AC9M6P01 | Match a digital model to a target probability | 5 |
| 16–17 | AC9M6P02 | Total relative frequencies and unequal-probability simulation | 5 |
| 18–20 | AC9M6P02 | Investigation design and careful interpretation | 6 |

Each form contains 18 four-option questions and two genuine random simulations. Simulations grow cumulatively through 10, 100 and 1000 trials, recording counts and percentages at each checkpoint. The controls stay above the results as the table grows. Students record the final counts; scores depend on their actual results, not a required split. No statement claims that every larger sample is necessarily closer to the prediction.

The shared lesson apparatus is reused. The numerical scale has no target highlighting. Fractions use the shared stacked formatter. Read-aloud narrates visible evidence and summarises simulation checkpoints without reading out 1000 individual trials or supplying hidden answers.

Validation includes all 100 items, both codes and all six lesson weeks, distinct answer choices, fraction/decimal answers, simulation sampling boundaries, expected totals, actual-result scoring, full regression audits, TypeScript/lint, all five forms in Chrome, small-screen checks and Levels 3–5 experiment regression.
