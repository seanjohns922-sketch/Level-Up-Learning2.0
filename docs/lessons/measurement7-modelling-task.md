# Level 7 Measurement — Modelling Task: Concrete Garden Path

**Week 12 · Teacher-marked · AC9M7M06 (with AC9M7M01, AC9M7M02)**

AC9M7M06 asks students to use mathematical modelling with ratios, formulate problems, interpret and communicate solutions, and *justify* the choices they made about the representation. Auto-marked questions cannot assess all of that, so this task sits beside the Week 12 lessons. It does not gate the post-test.

Allow about 50 minutes. Students may use a calculator or a spreadsheet.

---

## Student task

The school is laying a concrete path in the garden. The budget is **$300**.

**Design**

- The main path is a rectangle **6 m long and 0.9 m wide**.
- The garden club would also like a triangular seating pad at one end. Its base is the path's width, **0.9 m**, and its perpendicular height is **1.2 m**.
- Concrete is poured **10 cm (0.1 m) deep** everywhere.

**Concrete mix (by volume):** cement : sand : gravel = **1 : 2 : 3**

| Material | Bag size | Price per bag |
|---|---|---|
| Cement | 0.015 m³ | $12 |
| Sand | 0.02 m³ | $8 |
| Gravel | 0.02 m³ | $9 |

Only whole bags can be bought. Builders usually order **10% extra** concrete to allow for spills and uneven ground.

1. **Formulate.** What volume of concrete is needed for the path alone, and for the path plus the seating pad? State your assumptions.
2. **Represent and calculate.** Use the ratio to find how much of each material is needed, how many bags to buy, and the total cost. Do this for both designs, with and without the 10% extra.
3. **Interpret.** Which plans fit the $300 budget? Explain what rounding up to whole bags means in practice.
4. **Justify.** Recommend a plan to the garden club. Explain why you chose it and why the 1 : 2 : 3 ratio must be kept. Draw the path on a plan with scale 1 : 50 and label its plan length and width.

---

## Marking rubric (out of 12)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Formulate** | No usable model | Finds one volume | Finds both volumes (rectangle and triangle prism) | Both volumes, with assumptions stated (e.g. constant depth, whole bags) |
| **Represent and calculate** | Mostly incorrect | Some shares or bag counts correct | Shares, bags and costs correct for at least two plans | All four plans correct, with efficient use of the ratio |
| **Interpret** | No interpretation | States costs without reference to the budget | Identifies which plans fit $300 | Also explains leftover material from rounding up bags |
| **Justify and communicate** | No recommendation | Recommendation with no reason | Recommendation justified by cost and budget | Also justifies keeping the ratio, includes a correct 1 : 50 plan and names a limitation |

## Worked solution (for teachers)

**Volumes**

- Path: 6 × 0.9 × 0.1 = **0.54 m³**
- Seating pad: 0.9 × 1.2 ÷ 2 × 0.1 = **0.054 m³**
- Path + pad: **0.594 m³**

**Shares:** there are 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 parts, so cement is 1/6, sand 2/6 and gravel 3/6 of the volume.

| Plan | Volume | Cement (bags) | Sand (bags) | Gravel (bags) | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Path only | 0.54 m³ | 0.09 m³ → 6 → $72 | 0.18 m³ → 9 → $72 | 0.27 m³ → 13.5, so 14 → $126 | **$270** |
| Path + pad | 0.594 m³ | 0.099 m³ → 6.6, so 7 → $84 | 0.198 m³ → 9.9, so 10 → $80 | 0.297 m³ → 14.85, so 15 → $135 | **$299** |
| Path only + 10% | 0.594 m³ | 7 → $84 | 10 → $80 | 15 → $135 | **$299** |
| Path + pad + 10% | 0.6534 m³ | 0.1089 m³ → 7.26, so 8 → $96 | 0.2178 m³ → 10.89, so 11 → $88 | 0.3267 m³ → 16.34, so 17 → $153 | **$337** |

**Interpretation:** the pad is exactly 10% of the path's volume, so "path + pad" and "path + 10% extra" need the same concrete and both cost $299. Building the pad *and* ordering 10% extra costs $337, which is over budget.

**A strong recommendation** builds the path only, with 10% extra ($299), because running short of concrete mid-pour is costly. Alternatively, build the pad without spare concrete and accept the risk. Either is acceptable if it's justified. The ratio must be kept because changing it changes the concrete's strength.

**Plan at 1 : 50:** 600 cm ÷ 50 = **12 cm** long and 90 cm ÷ 50 = **1.8 cm** wide.

**Limitations to look for:** real concrete shrinks when mixed (the dry volume needed is larger); prices change; some leftover bags could be returned or reused.
