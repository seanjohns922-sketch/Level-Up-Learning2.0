# Level 7 Number — Modelling Task: Sausage Sizzle Fundraiser

**Week 12 · Teacher-marked · AC9M7N09 (with AC9M7N05, AC9M7N06)**

AC9M7N09 asks students to *formulate* problems, *choose representations and efficient calculation strategies*, *interpret and communicate* solutions in context, and *justify* the choices they made. Single-answer questions cannot assess all of that, so this task sits beside the Week 12 lessons. It does not gate the post-test.

Allow about 50 minutes. Students may use a calculator or a spreadsheet.

---

## Student task

The Year 7 leaders are running a sausage sizzle to raise money for camp. There are **220 Year 7 students**. You need to recommend a selling price.

**Price options and expected sales**

| Price per sausage | Expected share of students who buy one |
|---|---|
| $2.50 | 60% |
| $3.00 | 50% |

**Supplies**

| Item | Pack | Pack price |
|---|---|---|
| Sausages | 12 per pack | $9.60 |
| Bread | 20 slices per loaf | $2.80 |
| Sauce | serves 40 per bottle | $3.50 |
| Barbecue gas hire | one per event | $15.00 |

Each sausage uses one slice of bread and one serve of sauce. Supplies can only be bought in whole packs.

1. **Formulate.** For each price, how many sausages will you sell? List any assumptions you make.
2. **Represent and calculate.** For each price, find the number of packs of each item, the total cost, the revenue, the profit, and the profit as a percentage of cost.
3. **Interpret.** Check your answers are reasonable using an estimate. Explain what your rounding decisions mean in the real situation.
4. **Justify.** Recommend a price. Explain why, and say which representation (fraction, decimal or percentage) you chose for at least one calculation and why it was efficient. Describe one limitation of the model.

---

## Marking rubric (out of 12)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Formulate** | No usable model | Finds buyers for one price only | Finds buyers for both prices | Both prices, with assumptions stated (e.g. one sausage each, whole packs) |
| **Represent and calculate** | Mostly incorrect | Some costs or revenue correct | Cost, revenue and profit correct for both prices, with minor slips | All values correct, including profit % of cost, with efficient methods |
| **Interpret** | No interpretation | States answers without context | Explains rounding up packs in context | Also checks answers with an estimate and comments on leftover stock |
| **Justify and communicate** | No recommendation | Recommendation with no reason | Recommendation justified by profit | Also justifies a choice of representation and names a limitation of the model |

## Worked solution (for teachers)

**$2.50 price:** buyers = 220 × 0.6 = 132.

- Sausages: 132 ÷ 12 = 11 packs → 11 × $9.60 = $105.60
- Bread: 132 ÷ 20 = 6.6 → round up to 7 loaves → $19.60
- Sauce: 132 ÷ 40 = 3.3 → round up to 4 bottles → $14.00
- Gas: $15.00
- Total cost = $154.20 · Revenue = 132 × $2.50 = $330.00 · Profit = $175.80
- Profit % of cost = 175.80 ÷ 154.20 × 100 ≈ 114%

**$3.00 price:** buyers = 220 × 0.5 = 110.

- Sausages: 110 ÷ 12 ≈ 9.2 → round up to 10 packs → $96.00
- Bread: 110 ÷ 20 = 5.5 → round up to 6 loaves → $16.80
- Sauce: 110 ÷ 40 = 2.75 → round up to 3 bottles → $10.50
- Gas: $15.00
- Total cost = $138.30 · Revenue = 110 × $3.00 = $330.00 · Profit = $191.70
- Profit % of cost = 191.70 ÷ 138.30 × 100 ≈ 139%

**Recommendation:** $3.00. Revenue is the same, but fewer sales need fewer packs, so profit is $15.90 higher.

**Estimate check:** about 130 sausages at about $2.50 is about $325 of revenue, which is consistent with $330.

**Efficient representations:** 60% = 0.6, so 220 × 0.6 is quicker than finding 1% first. 50% = 1/2, so the number of buyers is half of 220.

**Limitations to look for:** expected sales are guesses; some students may buy two sausages; leftover stock (e.g. 2 spare sausages at $3.00) could be sold or used later; vegetarian options are ignored.

## Spreadsheet extension

Put the number of buyers in a cell and use formulas such as `=ROUNDUP(B2/12,0)*9.6` for the sausage cost. Keep the $2.50 option at 132 buyers ($175.80 profit). How few buyers can the $3.00 option have and still make more profit? (Answer: 102 buyers, about 46% of students, gives $177.30. At 101 buyers the profit drops below $175.80.)
